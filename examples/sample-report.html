Why it matters: The server reports a version with a published security problem. Version numbers can be hidden or kept old while fixes are applied, so this needs confirming with your host.

The certificate expires in 9 days

Why it matters: When the certificate expires, browsers show a full-page warning and most visitors leave.

What to do: Ask your host to renew it before that date and to turn on automatic renewal.

Browsers are not told to always use the secure version (HSTS)

Why it matters: Without this setting, someone on the same network can push a visitor onto an unencrypted connection before the secure page loads.

What to do: Ask for the Strict-Transport-Security header with a max-age of at least 15552000 (180 days). Only turn it on once the whole site works over https.

Technical detail: header missing

Other sites can show your pages inside theirs

Why it matters: A fake site can load your page invisibly and trick visitors into clicking buttons on it, such as "buy" or "delete". This is called clickjacking.

What to do: Ask for the header X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN, or frame-ancestors 'self' in the content security policy.

Technical detail: no X-Frame-Options or frame-ancestors
Recommendedrosas-bakery.example

Some cookies are missing protection flags

Why it matters: Cookies without the Secure flag can travel over unencrypted connections, and cookies without HttpOnly can be read by scripts on the page. Login cookies need both.

What to do: Ask your developer to set the Secure and HttpOnly flags on cookies, plus SameSite=Lax.

Technical detail: without Secure: PHPSESSID

The server reveals its software version

Why it matters: Showing the exact version tells attackers which known weaknesses to try first.

What to do: Ask your host to hide the version number (for example server_tokens off in nginx, or ServerTokens Prod in Apache).

Technical detail: Apache/2.4.41 (Ubuntu)
Tiprosas-bakery.example

The site announces the technology behind it (X-Powered-By)

Why it matters: Announcing the technology and its version helps attackers pick what to try.

What to do: Ask your developer to remove the X-Powered-By header (for PHP: expose_php = Off).

Technical detail: PHP/8.2.20
Tiprosas-bakery.example

No content security policy

Why it matters: This setting limits which scripts may run on your pages. It is the main defense if harmful code gets in, for example through a hacked plugin or a comment form.

What to do: Ask your developer to add a Content-Security-Policy header. It needs care so the site keeps working, so start with the report-only version.

Technical detail: header missing
Tiprosas-bakery.example

No referrer policy is set

Why it matters: When visitors click a link to another site, the full address of your page can be passed along, sometimes with private details in it.

What to do: Ask for the header Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin. Modern browsers already behave this way by default, so this is low priority.

Technical detail: header missing

What this check-up covered

  • Whether visitors are sent to the secure (https) version of the site.
  • The security settings the site sends with its pages: HSTS, content security policy, protection against framing, file-type guessing, referrer policy and cookie flags.
  • Software versions the server reveals, compared with published security problems.
  • The site's certificate: whether it is trusted, when it expires, its key strength and the encryption version.

Not covered

  • Pages behind a login, and anything that needs a password.
  • Attempts to break in (a penetration test).
  • Plugins, themes and other software the server does not announce.
  • Live certificate revocation checks.
  • Anything not listed on the signed permission form.
This report covers only the checks listed above and the addresses on the permission form, at the time of the check. A clean result is not a guarantee that a site is secure. Prepared by Your Name, 23 October 2026.
